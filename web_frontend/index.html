The problem

A guard can't watch forty screens at once.

Most CCTV systems only become useful after something has already happened — someone has to pull the tape. Sentinel is built to catch the moment a weapon appears, not just record it: it detects the object, locks onto the person holding it, and keeps that identity alive as they move out of one camera's view and into another's.

Pipeline

How a single frame becomes an alert

Every frame from every connected camera moves through the same six stages, end to end, in real time.

01

Capture

Pulls frames from a webcam, an RTSP/CCTV stream, or an uploaded recording — same pipeline either way.

02

Pose & weapon detection

A YOLOv12s model trained on gun/knife footage runs alongside a YOLO‑Pose skeleton model on every person.

03

Wrist–weapon association

Each detected weapon is matched to the nearest wrist keypoint to decide who is holding it, not just that one exists in frame.

04

Suspect snapshot

The carrier's crop is saved and passed through an OSNet Re‑ID model to generate a 512‑d appearance embedding.

05

Cross‑camera re‑ID

New embeddings are cosine‑matched against the suspect database, so the same person is recognised on a different camera.

06

Evidence & alert

Every detection and re‑match is timestamped to an evidence log, and the live dashboard raises the alert.

Three models, one dashboard

What's actually running

Two independent pipelines feed the same live dashboard — one watches for weapons, the other reads crowd behaviour.

◆

Weapon detection

Custom YOLOv12s detector, trained from scratch on 8,807 annotated frames across two classes — pistol and knife — pulled from real CCTV‑angle footage.

YOLOv12s 60 epochs mAP50 0.834
◎

Suspect re‑identification

A YOLO‑Pose skeleton links the weapon to a wrist, the carrier is cropped, and an OSNet embedding lets the system recognise that same person again on a completely different camera.

YOLO11‑Pose OSNet (torchreid) Cosine similarity
▦

Activity & crowd heatmap

A second pose pipeline classifies every tracked person as sitting, standing, walking or running, while a DeepSORT‑based tracker groups people and renders a live movement heatmap of the scene.

YOLO11‑Pose DeepSORT Heatmap overlay
